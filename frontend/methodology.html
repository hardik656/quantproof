01
Overfitting Detection
22% of Fundable Score · 9 checks
The most common failure mode in backtesting. A strategy that was optimised on historical data will always look good — the question is whether it has real predictive power or just memorised the past. These checks test whether the edge survives scrutiny.
CORESharpe Ratio Decay
Splits the backtest in half and compares in-sample vs out-of-sample Sharpe ratio. Also checks mean return decay independently. Uses the worse of the two as the effective decay score.
A strategy with good in-sample and bad out-of-sample performance was fitted to noise. Real edge decays slowly, not catastrophically.
COREMonte Carlo Robustness
Three-component test: (1) bootstrap mean stability across 300 resamples, (2) sequence independence across 200 permutations, (3) worst-5th-percentile equity curve across 500 permutations.
A strategy that only works because of the specific order of trades is not robust. This checks whether the edge holds when the sequence changes.
COREProfit / Loss Concentration
Measures what percentage of total profit comes from the top 1% of trades and the top 2 individual trades. Also checks loss concentration on the downside. Removes the top trades and recalculates Sharpe.
A strategy that depends on 2-3 outlier trades is not a strategy — it is luck. Remove those trades and the Sharpe should remain positive.
ADVANCEDCPCV Path Stability
Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation with 6 splits and 2 test folds. Applies embargo gaps to prevent data leakage. Generates multiple walk-forward paths and measures path Sharpe standard deviation.
Standard train/test splits leak information at the boundary. CPCV with purging and embargo prevents this and gives a realistic distribution of out-of-sample performance.
Academic Reference
Marcos López de Prado — "Advances in Financial Machine Learning" (2018), Ch.12
Bailey & López de Prado — "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (2014)
COREBootstrap Stability
500 bootstrap resamples of the trade sequence. Reports the percentage with positive mean return.
A robust edge shows positive expectancy in the vast majority of resampled universes, not just the original sequence.
ADVANCEDDeflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)
Corrects the Sharpe ratio for non-normality (skewness and excess kurtosis), finite sample bias, and multiple testing. Uses the Harvey-Liu-Zhu framework to estimate the minimum Sharpe required for statistical significance given the number of strategies tested.
A raw Sharpe ratio of 1.5 on 100 trades after testing 20 strategies is statistically meaningless. DSR expresses the true probability that the Sharpe is genuine.
Academic Reference
Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu — "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (2014), J. Portfolio Management
Harvey, Liu & Zhu — "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns" (2016), Review of Financial Studies
CORESharpe Confidence Interval
Computes the 95% confidence interval for the annualised Sharpe using the Mertens (2002) standard error formula, corrected for skewness and kurtosis. Reports the lower CI bound — this is the pessimistic estimate of the real Sharpe.
A Sharpe of 1.5 on 30 trades has a CI so wide it includes zero. The lower CI bound is what matters for deployment decisions.
COREMinimum Backtest Length
Computes the Harvey-Liu-Zhu minimum observations required for statistical significance at the strategy's Sharpe level, adjusted for the number of strategies tested.
Short backtests always overfit. This check quantifies exactly how many trades you need before the results are credible.
ADVANCEDAutocorrelation (Ljung-Box + Newey-West)
Tests for serial correlation in returns using the Ljung-Box Q-statistic at lags 1, 5, and 10. If significant autocorrelation is detected, computes Newey-West HAC standard error and quantifies the inflation factor vs the IID assumption.
All other statistical tests (DSR, CI, Bootstrap) assume returns are independent. Serial correlation — common with look-ahead bias or return smoothing — invalidates those tests and overstates significance.
Academic Reference
Ljung & Box — "On a Measure of Lack of Fit in Time Series Models" (1978), Biometrika 65(2)
Newey & West — "A Simple Positive Semi-Definite Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent Covariance Matrix" (1987), Econometrica 55(3)
Lo — "The Statistics of Sharpe Ratios" (2002), Financial Analysts Journal
02
Risk Management
38% of Fundable Score · 11 checks
The largest category by weight because risk management is the primary reason strategies fail in live trading. A strategy can have genuine alpha and still blow up due to position sizing, tail risk, or extended drawdowns.
Max Drawdown
Maximum peak-to-trough equity curve decline as a percentage. Institutional threshold: <20%. Prop firm threshold: <10%.
Every prop firm and institutional fund has a hard drawdown limit. Exceeding it means termination of the account or mandate.
Calmar Ratio
Compound Annual Growth Rate divided by Maximum Drawdown. Uses geometric annualisation (CAGR), not arithmetic mean. Threshold: >1.5.
Measures return per unit of drawdown risk. A strategy with high returns but equal drawdown is not efficient — the Calmar ratio catches this.
Value at Risk (VaR 99%)
99th percentile loss expressed as a ratio to strategy volatility (VaR/σ). A normal distribution gives VaR/σ ≈ 2.33. Ratios above 4.0σ signal extreme fat tails beyond what vol-targeting can manage.
Absolute VaR without context is meaningless. Normalising by volatility reveals whether the tail risk is proportional to the strategy's normal behaviour.
CVaR / Expected Shortfall
Average loss in the worst 5% of outcomes. Uses kernel density estimation for small samples (<100 trades). CVaR is the risk measure used by Basel III for bank capital requirements.
VaR tells you the threshold. CVaR tells you what happens when you cross it — the average magnitude of tail losses.
Sortino Ratio
Return divided by downside deviation only. Uses the correct Sortino & van der Meer (1991) formula: downside deviation = sqrt(mean(min(r,0)²)) using ALL returns, not just negative ones.
Sharpe penalises upside volatility equally with downside. Sortino only penalises harmful volatility — more appropriate for asymmetric strategies.
Max Losing Streak
Longest consecutive sequence of losing trades. Also flags zero losing streaks over 20+ trades as implausible — a known signature of look-ahead bias or synthetic data.
Most prop firms terminate accounts after 5-8 consecutive losses. Knowing your worst streak lets you size positions to survive it psychologically and financially.
Recovery Factor
Ratio of total gross profits to total gross losses. Threshold: >1.5 — meaning wins must generate at least 50% more than losses cost.
A strategy where wins barely cover losses has no margin for the inevitable variance. Drawdowns become permanent rather than temporary.
Absolute Sharpe Ratio
Annualised Sharpe ratio using sample standard deviation (ddof=1) with correct annualisation for the strategy's actual trade frequency. Institutional minimum: 1.0.
The baseline risk-adjusted return measure. Required by every CTA, hedge fund, and prop firm as a minimum quality gate.
ADVANCEDProbability of Ruin
Generalised gambler's ruin formula at institutional position sizing: N=50 capital units (2% risk per trade). Uses reward-to-risk ratio in the ruin calculation, not just win rate.
At 2% risk per trade, a genuinely profitable strategy should have near-zero ruin probability. If it doesn't, the edge is insufficient for real money deployment.
Academic Reference
Vince — "The Mathematics of Money Management" (1992)
Thorp — "The Kelly Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting and the Stock Market" (2008)
Drawdown Duration & Recovery
Time spent in the maximum drawdown, scaled to backtest length. Threshold scales from 180 days minimum up to 730 days (2 years) for long multi-year backtests. Checks whether the strategy ever recovered from the worst drawdown.
A 30-day prop firm challenge and a 7-year institutional backtest have different acceptable drawdown durations. Fixed thresholds penalise long backtests unfairly.
Ulcer Index
Root mean square of all drawdown percentages throughout the backtest. Combines drawdown depth and duration into one number. Also reports the Martin Ratio (Sharpe / Ulcer Index).
Max drawdown only captures the single worst event. The Ulcer Index captures the overall pain experienced throughout the entire backtest period.
03
Regime Robustness
14% of Fundable Score · 6 checks
A strategy backtested entirely in a bull market is not tested — it is lucky. These checks verify that the strategy works across different market conditions, not just the period when everything went up.
Bull / Bear / Consolidation Performance
When date data is present, classifies trades using actual market regime periods (S&P 500 bull/bear cycles from 2000–2026). Without dates, uses rolling return proxy. Computes Sharpe and win rate for each regime separately.
Most retail backtests run from 2019-2024 — almost entirely a bull market. Performance in bear and consolidation regimes reveals the real durability of the edge.
ADVANCEDVolatility Spike Stress Test
Simulates a 3x volatility spike using Student's t-distribution (fat tails, df=3) with AR(1) autocorrelation to model crisis momentum. Strategy-specific seed ensures different strategies get different stress paths. Measures Sharpe degradation and stress drawdown.
Volatility regimes change abruptly. A strategy calibrated to normal vol can be destroyed when vol triples — which happens in every major crisis.
Performance Consistency
Percentage of rolling windows with positive mean return throughout the backtest.
A strategy that is profitable on average but has extended unprofitable periods is difficult to trade live — the drawdown periods test psychological resilience and capital availability.
Regime Coverage
Checks whether the backtest data includes a regime column covering multiple market conditions (BULL, BEAR, CONSOLIDATION, TRANSITION).
Explicit regime labelling enables regime-conditional position sizing in live trading — a critical tool for managing drawdown across market cycles.
04
Execution Reality
11% of Fundable Score · 6 checks
Backtests assume perfect fills. Reality does not. These checks model the gap between backtest performance and live performance due to slippage, commissions, partial fills, and market impact.
Slippage Impact (0.1% and 0.3%)
Two separate checks. 0.1% represents institutional / large-cap stocks. 0.3% represents retail / small-cap / illiquid markets. Both scale with trade frequency — high-frequency strategies face larger effective slippage per unit of return.
Slippage is often larger than the edge. A strategy with 0.2% mean return that fails the 0.1% slippage test has no real edge after costs.
Commission Drag
Three commission tiers based on trade frequency: 2bps institutional (≤252 trades/yr), 10bps retail equity (≤2520/yr), 20bps HFT/spread (>2520/yr). Measures commission as a percentage of gross turnover.
Commission cost scales with frequency. High-frequency strategies pay proportionally more — this check ensures the strategy's gross edge survives realistic commission rates.
Partial Fill Simulation
Models 80% fill rate — common in illiquid markets and during fast-moving sessions. Estimates the resulting return reduction.
Most backtests assume 100% fills. In reality, limit orders miss, market orders slip, and fast markets leave orders partially or completely unfilled.
Live Trading Gap Estimate
Applies a 40% Sharpe degradation from backtest to live, based on empirical research on live vs backtest performance across retail strategies.
Every strategy loses some edge when deployed live due to execution, emotion, and data differences. A strategy needs sufficient backtest Sharpe to still be viable after this degradation.
ADVANCEDImpact-Adjusted Capacity
Almgren-Chriss market impact model. Computes strategy Sharpe at $100k, $1M, $10M, and $100M AUM. Uses the strategy's actual volatility and trade frequency to estimate market impact at each scale.
A strategy that works with $50k in capital may be completely destroyed at $5M due to market impact. Capacity analysis determines the maximum deployable capital before the edge disappears.
Academic Reference
Almgren & Chriss — "Optimal Execution of Portfolio Transactions" (2000), J. Risk 3(2)
05
Compliance
15% of Fundable Score · 3 checks
Two separate compliance standards: prop firm (FTMO/Topstep/The5ers challenge rules) and institutional (CTA/fund). These have different thresholds and serve different purposes — a strategy can pass institutional and fail prop firm, or vice versa.
Prop Firm Compliance (FTMO / Topstep / The5ers)
Checks compliance with all three major prop firm challenge rules: max drawdown ≤10%, profit target ≥6-10% depending on firm, Sharpe ≥1.0. Reports which firms the strategy is eligible for.
Each prop firm has slightly different rules. Knowing which you qualify for — and which violations you need to fix — saves time before paying for a challenge.
Institutional Compliance (CTA / Fund Standard)
Checks the institutional standard for multi-year strategies: Sharpe ≥1.0, max drawdown <25%, Calmar ≥1.0. More appropriate than prop firm rules for long backtests where a 10% drawdown limit is unrealistic.
A 5-year institutional backtest should not be judged by 30-day prop firm challenge rules. These are the actual standards used by fund allocators to screen managed accounts.
06
Historical Crash Simulations
Informational — do not affect Fundable Score
Five historical crisis scenarios simulated using fat-tailed Student's t-distribution with AR(1) crisis autocorrelation. Each strategy gets a unique simulation path based on its own returns data — the same crash hits different strategies differently based on their actual volatility and autocorrelation profile.
2008 Global Financial Crisis
Sep 2008 – Mar 2009
S&P 500 −56%. VIX hit 80. Correlations spiked to 1. Leverage unwinding forced selling across all asset classes simultaneously.
2020 COVID Crash
Feb 2020 – Mar 2020
Fastest 30%+ drop in market history — 34% in 33 days. Circuit breakers triggered 4 times. Then one of the fastest recoveries ever seen.
2022 Rate Hike Bear Market
Jan 2022 – Dec 2022
Fed raised rates 425bps in 12 months. Momentum strategies that crushed 2021 were destroyed. Growth stocks fell 60–90%.
2010 Flash Crash
May 6, 2010
Dow dropped 1000 points in minutes from algorithmic cascade. Liquidity vanished — many stocks traded at $0.01. HFT strategies blown out in seconds.
1998 LTCM / Russia Default
Aug–Sep 1998
Russia defaulted; LTCM collapsed under hidden leverage. Correlation assumptions broke simultaneously. Strategies with similar factor exposures were destroyed together.
Crash simulations are illustrative — they show stress exposure, not predictions. Strategies without date data receive [UNCALIBRATED] labels because market regime cannot be verified. Strategies with high AR(1) autocorrelation receive a 30% exposure multiplier — autocorrelated strategies experience harder unwind when trends reverse.
07
Plausibility Checks
4 checks — flag data integrity issues, can trigger Manual Audit Required
These checks do not contribute to the weighted Fundable Score. They flag statistical signatures that commonly indicate data errors, look-ahead bias, or synthetic data. A Manual Audit Required flag suppresses the Prop Firm Eligible result and caps the score at 35.
Sharpe Plausibility
Sharpe >10 triggers Manual Audit Required. Sharpe >5 triggers Review Recommended. No documented institutional strategy has sustained Sharpe >3.0 over 5+ years.
Frequency-Return Plausibility
HFT strategies (>20,000 trades/year) with extreme annual returns (>500%) require impossible liquidity and are flagged as implausible.
Equity Curve Smoothness
Suspiciously smooth equity curves with very low max drawdown (<2%) are a known signature of look-ahead bias or data errors.
Kelly Plausibility
Kelly fraction >1.0 (bet more than your entire bankroll on every trade) is mathematically impossible in a real strategy. Flags data errors or unrealistic win/loss distributions.
